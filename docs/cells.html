Cells

First the settings every cell shares, then each kind of cell in turn: solar, house, grid, home battery, car charger.

The shape of a cell

A cell has four groups of settings. The wizard shows them on one screen; conceptually they answer four different questions.

GroupAnswers
Top levelWhere does it sit and what is it allowed to do?
HardwareWhat are its physical limits and which sensors measure it?
AlgorithmHow aggressively should it be used?
ActuatorHow do I tell it what to do?

Top level

SettingDefaultRangeMeaning
id—text Short name. Becomes part of every entity id for this cell, so choose it once and keep it: goodwe, car, marstek.
position20–99 Priority. Lower is served first. Equal position means the cells form a pool.
modeoff off · surplus · balanced · charge · discharge · autonomous The policy. See Concepts.
typeempty empty · house_battery · car_charger · grid What kind of thing this is. Drives the dashboard grouping and a few type-specific behaviours.

Hardware

SettingDefaultRangeMeaning
can_chargetrueyes/no Whether this device can absorb energy at all.
can_dischargetrueyes/no Whether it can give energy back. A car charger is normally charge-only.
max_charge_w50000–25000 Physical charge ceiling, in watts. Never exceeded, in any mode.
max_discharge_w50000–25000 Physical discharge ceiling, in watts.
capacity_kwh100–200 Usable capacity. Used for the pool split and for the energy accounting, so an honest figure matters more than a datasheet one.
min_soc100–100 Never discharge below this. The discharge taper starts easing off as the cell approaches it, rather than slamming to zero.
max_soc950–100 Never charge above this, with the same taper on the way up.
power_sensor—entity Measured power for this cell, in watts.
power_sign11 or −1 Set to −1 if your sensor reports charging as negative. This is the one place a sign may be flipped, precisely so it does not have to be flipped elsewhere.
soc_sensor—entity State of charge, in percent. Required for anything storage-like.
energy_charged_sensor—entity Lifetime charged energy in kWh, for the accounting totals.
energy_discharged_sensor—entity Lifetime discharged energy in kWh.
self_consumption_w00–5000 Standing draw of the device itself — inverter idle load, charger standby. Accepts an entity id.

Set min_soc at or above max_soc and the pair is rejected and reset to 10/95, with a warning in the log. An inverted band would break both tapers, so it is not allowed to stand.

Algorithm

SettingDefaultRangeMeaning
take_pct1000–100 How much of what this cell is offered it may actually take. The main dial for sharing surplus between competing devices. Accepts an entity id.
hysteresis30–50 Dead band around the target state of charge, in percent, so the cell does not flip between charging and discharging at the boundary.
charge_floor_w00–10000 Below this many watts, do not bother charging. Stops a device being switched on for a trickle it cannot use efficiently.
min_soc_modebalanced balanced · manual balanced runs the floor algorithm with its safeguards. manual takes manual_min_soc verbatim, which is how you deliberately go lower than the safeguards would allow.
sunny_min_soc250–100 Floor to hold when a good solar day is expected. Lower, because the sun will refill it.
no_sun_min_soc400–100 Floor to hold when little sun is expected. Higher, because it will not.
manual_min_soc150–100 The verbatim floor, used only when min_soc_mode is manual.
pv_weight00–100 This cell's share of the PV array, when several cells sit behind different strings. Normalised across cells.
target_soc—0–100 Fixed target instead of a computed one. Leave empty to let the algorithm decide.
connected_sensor—entity Is the device physically connected? A car charger with no car should not be offered surplus.
charging_sensor—entity Is it actually drawing? Validating against delivered current rather than a reported status catches chargers that claim to be charging while delivering nothing.
charge_mode_entity—entity An entity carrying the device's own charge mode, when it has one.
set_flag_on_chargeemptyentity id A flag to raise while this cell is charging. The other half of the interlock below.
no_discharge_on_flagemptyentity id Do not discharge while that flag is on. The classic use: the car raises a flag while charging, and the house battery refuses to discharge into it.

no_discharge_on_flag is an interlock, not a preference. discharge mode does not override it.

Actuator

This is the seam between Energy Balancer and your hardware. The integration hands over a direction and a number; everything vendor-specific lives in the script you name here.

SettingDefaultMeaning
serviceempty The script or service to call, for example script.eb_actuator_goodwe. Leave empty and the cell is observed but never steered.
unitW What your device expects: W, A or pct. Conversion is done for you.
voltage230 Used for the watts-to-amps conversion. Range 100–400.
phases1 1 or 3. Also used for the amps conversion.

Write your actuator script so every path leaves the device in a known state. If one branch puts the hardware into a forced mode, every other branch must be able to lift it. A script that forces on the charge path but does not release on the discharge path leaves the device stuck the moment the decision changes — and it will look, from the integration's side, exactly like it worked.

Cell by cell

Solar

Structural, mode supply. It contributes its measured production to rest and takes nothing. There is nothing to configure beyond the production sensor on the system screen, and nothing to actuate — you cannot tell the sun what to do.

It still gets its own device and entities, so production shows up in the chain view and in the accounting like anything else.

House

Structural, mode demand. It subtracts its measured consumption from rest. This is the load nobody steers: lights, fridge, oven, everything that simply happens.

Getting this figure right matters more than any tuning parameter. If it includes a device that is also a cell, the chain double-counts and hands out surplus that was already spent. Use house_includes to subtract the overlap.

Grid

Structural, mode grid. Always last. It absorbs whatever the chain could not place — exporting a surplus nobody wanted, importing a deficit nobody covered.

Its one constraint is the import limit, and that constraint is what travels back up the chain as headroom. Everything upstream is bounded by it.

Home battery

Type house_battery. The most configurable cell, and the one the pooling logic exists for.

Typical settings

positionThe same on every battery you want pooled.
modebalanced for forecast-driven behaviour, surplus for plain self-consumption.
capacity_kwhHonest usable capacity — this drives the pool split.
min_soc / max_socThe physical band you are willing to use.
soc_sensorRequired. Without it the cell cannot participate in a pool.

With two batteries at the same position, the state-of-charge target becomes a capacity-weighted average across both, and per-cell targets stop being meaningful. That is why the sliders for sunny_min_soc, no_sun_min_soc and manual_min_soc become pool-level: one knob, written to every member.

Car charger

Type car_charger. Usually charge-only, and usually the cell whose position you will move around the most.

Typical settings

can_dischargefalse, unless you have bidirectional charging.
modesurplus — charge from what is left over.
charge_floor_wWorth setting. Most chargers will not start below roughly 1.4 kW anyway.
connected_sensorSo a charger with no car does not get offered surplus.
charging_sensorPrefer one based on delivered current over one based on a reported status.
set_flag_on_chargeRaise a flag the house battery can refuse to discharge into.
actuator.unitOften A — many chargers take amps, not watts.

Put the charger before the batteries and the car gets surplus first. Put it after and the batteries fill first. Both are legitimate; the difference is whether you would rather drive on sun or keep the house on sun after dark.
