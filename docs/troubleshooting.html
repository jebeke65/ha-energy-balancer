Troubleshooting

Start with the three checks below. They account for most of what looks like a bug.

The first three checks

  1. Is observer mode still on? switch.eb_observer_mode. It is on by default and it means nothing is actuated. This is the single most common cause of "it computes but does nothing".
  2. Are all sensors available? sensor.eb_diag_sensors_unavailable should read zero. Anything else and the chain is working with holes in its inputs.
  3. Is the grid sign right? sensor.eb_diag_grid_sign. Import must be positive. Get this wrong and surplus reads as deficit — the chain will charge when it should discharge, and every other symptom follows from it.

A battery will not charge

CauseHow to see it
Already at max_socCompare its state of charge to the configured ceiling. The taper eases charging off before the limit, so it slows down first.
No surplus, and the mode is surplussensor.eb_surplus_total_w. In surplus mode a battery is not supposed to import.
No peak headroomsensor.eb_current_vs_peak_percentage. Charging is bounded by the import ceiling in every mode, forced ones included.
Below charge_floor_wThe offered power is too small to be worth switching on for.
take_pct at zeroCheck the slider, and check the entity behind it if it points at one.
An external charge policy says noLook at the mode attribute of your charge_pct_sensor and its stated reason.
The device did not obeyCompare its power sensor with the decision on sensor.eb_<cell>. If they disagree, the fault is in the actuator script or the device, not in the chain.

A device stuck in a forced mode looks exactly like a working one. If your actuator script puts hardware into a forced or manual state on the charge path, every other path must be able to lift it again. When it cannot, the chain will keep issuing correct decisions and the device will keep ignoring them — quietly, with nothing in the log. Check the device's own mode registers before you suspect the chain.

A battery will not discharge

CauseHow to see it

can_discharge is falseCheck the cell's hardware settings.

The house imports while a battery is full

Usually one of three things:

  • A pool average hiding an empty member. A pool at 15% can be one battery at 25% and one at 5%. The pool looks able; half of it is not. Check the members individually.
  • "Do not charge" was executed as "do nothing". A zero from an external charge policy means stop importing, not park the battery. If the actuator script reads it as "stand still", the house ends up on the grid with storage sitting idle beside it.
  • A leftover ceiling. If your script clamps a maximum power on the charge path and never lifts it, the device's own regulator can never ramp past whatever number was last written.

Values look wrong

House consumption too high

Your house sensor probably includes a device that is also a cell, so it is counted twice. List that entity under house_includes and it will be subtracted.

Everything reads zero or unavailable

Check sensor.eb_diag_sensors_unavailable, then the log. Every rejected parameter is logged once, by name, with the value that was rejected and the default that replaced it.

Power flips sign

Set power_sign to −1 on that cell. Do it there and nowhere else — a sign corrected in two places is a sign that will eventually be wrong in one of them.

A slider does nothing

The parameter is probably configured as an entity reference, which makes that entity the single knob. Change the entity, not the slider. If a slider exists at all, the value was numeric.

The pool split looks unfair

It is weighted by remaining room: the distance from each cell's state of charge to its ceiling, times its capacity in kWh. The emptiest and largest battery takes the largest share. If the split looks wrong, check the configured capacities first — an optimistic capacity_kwh skews every split it takes part in.

The pool broke apart

Pooling requires every cell at that position to be in surplus or balanced. Force one member to charge or discharge and the pool dissolves; the members are handled one after another instead. This is deliberate — a pool with a forced member is not a pool.

Turning on the logs

logger:
  default: warning
  logs:
    custom_components.energy_balancer: debug

Every tick is then logged with its inputs, the decision per cell and the reason for it. Turn it off again afterwards — ten-second ticks fill a log quickly.

Reporting a problem

Open an issue at the issue tracker with:

  • Home Assistant and Energy Balancer versions.
  • Your cell setup: positions, modes, types.
  • The state and attributes of sensor.eb_status and of the cell concerned.
  • What you expected, and what happened instead.
  • Debug log covering the moment it went wrong.
